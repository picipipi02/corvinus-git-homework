    AGV / AMRNot used: side loaders and conveyors cover the internal transport

    PickingNo item picking: whole pallets are retrieved, unpacked into boxes and sorted by conveyor
    ITWMS + handheld RF scanners; boxes scanned at unboxing so the conveyor routes each to the right trucking station

    Ideas to tighten the design

    • Balance the two unboxing stations. With one station per 2 rows, rows 1–2 and 3–4 need similar volumes, or one station queues while the other waits. Put fast movers (ABC "A" items) at the rack ends nearest unboxing.
    • Side loaders do double duty. They handle both put-away and retrieval, so check that 3–4 trucks cover the peak hour.
    • Use the height. The building is 10 m clear but the racks stop at 6.5 m. A 5th pallet level adds 25% capacity on the same floor, as long as sprinkler clearance is kept.
    • Check the truck type. Side loaders are mainly for long loads. Reach trucks or VNA (turret) trucks are the usual choice for pallets in narrow aisles.
    • Add a returns area. Clothing has a high return rate, so returns need their own inspect-and-restock space near receiving.

    Assumptions

    • The sketch has no scale. We assume 1 grid square = 2 m, which gives 66 × 36 m.
    • Sizes from the sheet: pallet rack 1 × 2.7 m, 6.5 m high; flow rack 2 × 2 m, 2.5 m high.
    • The flow racks are pallet flow racks used as the hand-over buffer: forklifts load from the receiving side, side loaders unload from the rack side.
    • The 10 boxes at the conveyor ends are the trucking stations (one per truck / route); boxes are collected in roll cages there.
    • Shelving (0.5 × 1 m) is listed on the sheet but not drawn, so it isn't modelled.
